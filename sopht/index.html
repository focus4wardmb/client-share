01 · What we heard

Where Sopht is, in your words

The channel is a real bet. A global partnership with Infosys, phase 3 co-marketing ahead. Yet the LVMH deal started direct: the operational team pulled EY in, EY pulled Sopht in. The channel carries deals it didn't originate.

The champion is a Green IT manager with a small budget. When budgets tighten, that line is the first one questioned. Sales then struggles to show ROI in numbers the customer will sign off, in percent or in euros.

Happy customers buy something the site doesn't sell. Automated data collection, help getting the data clean, a methodology at the state of the art. Clean data is the friction Sopht removes, and it appears nowhere in the current story.

The product was never carbon-only. Full IT estate from day one, cost in the product since 2023. Carbon led because it opened doors in 2022.

What May already showed still holds. Three category words on three surfaces (ITOps, GreenOps, Green IT). Enterprise logos as the strongest asset, messaging leading with features. The regulatory tailwind unused. The brief adds the pivot itself: a cost-first story live on the site since late 2025 that the master deck and the sales team don't yet carry, and that no customer has yet heard.

02 · Two gaps

What the brief doesn't cover

The brief is strong on competitors and honest about its doubts. It is silent on the two things that decide a category.

What the channel can carry. A Capgemini consultant recommends Sopht inside their own engagement with no Sopht person in the room. The narrative's hardest job is surviving being retold by someone who doesn't work for you. The brief's persona list has no integrator in it.

The one question that resolves most of it: when Capgemini or EY brings Sopht into an account, what is the program called on their side, and which practice does the consultant sit in?

How analysts frame the shelf. Gartner and Forrester name categories, and they work with the same integrators. If Sopht's story matches how they see the market moving, the wind is behind it. If not, every sales conversation starts with a translation. A CEO conversation, and already on Jérémie's radar.

03 · Five questions

Before choosing a category

No conclusions here. These are the questions the work would answer, with evidence, in the first three weeks.

01
New buyer, or bigger business case?

Customers said carbon alone doesn't fund a budget. They did not say "become a cost tool." One means changing who you sell to. The other means handing the champion you have the arithmetic their CIO needs to sign.

02
Which fight can a 40-person company win?

Cost-first puts Sopht on a shelf with Apptio under IBM, Flexera and Broadcom. Sustainability-first puts it next to Verdikt and Fruggr, with cooler demand. The brief's own hunch, scope plus operational action plus auditable method, may be the third option.

03
Did regulation disappear, or move?

The Omnibus pushed CSRD back two years and took the mid-market out of scope. Sopht's enterprise customers are still fully in, REEN still stands, and AI is adding energy demand nobody planned for.

04
Is "budget" an objection, or a timing?

A renewal under pressure is a delay, not a stop, if sales can show what stops when Sopht stops. The three saved renewals hold the arguments. Worth writing down before they're forgotten.

05
Whose decision is this?

Category is not a marketing deliverable. If sales sells A, product builds B and marketing says C, no story survives. Made once, with sales and product in the room.

04 · Shape of the work

How the mission would run

The brief's shape fits: one day a week, mid-September to early November, English first. What changes is the front end: three weeks of evidence before a word of narrative is written.

ElementStatusHow
Product strengthPartly in the briefWhat Sopht can credibly own: full-estate modelling, simulation, auditable method
ChannelNot coveredTwo conversations with integrator consultants who carry Sopht today
AnalystsNot coveredHow Gartner, Forrester and IDC frame the shelf, and where Sopht lands
Market and competitorsWell coveredVerified at source

Five to six conversations in weeks one to three: two integrator consultants, two or three customers, one lost deal. The category memo becomes the spine. Then, in order: master narrative, message hierarchy, messaging per persona including the integrator, structure and copy of the new master deck.

One deliverable the brief didn't ask for. The team already runs AI skills on product, market and tone of voice. They'll be out of date the day the new story lands. So the positioning ships as a Claude-ready GTM Brain: narrative, vocabulary, competitive read and voice of customer in one repository every future draft starts from. It's how the work keeps running after the mission ends.
