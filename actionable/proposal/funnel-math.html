From Actionable's targets, backwards.

The 2027 plan says: €3M ARR at the end of 2026, €7M at the end of 2027. This page turns that company goal into marketing numbers: what €7M is made of, what marketing must feed, where demos come from, and what it costs. Every figure is Actionable's own data (2027 ARR plan, S1 2026 sales review) or a named benchmark. Ranges, not promises: we recalibrate on the first 30-60 days of real activity.

So marketing's number is not €7M. It's the €2.8M of new business, and the pipeline that produces it: the plan's own rule is ×3 coverage → €8.5M of new pipeline to create in 2027, €2.1M per quarter. S1 2026 created €1.57M in six months, €0.78M per quarter: the plan asks for 2.7x today's pace. (×3 coverage implies one pipeline euro in three closes; S1's actual was 26% of opportunities signed. The plan itself lists this assumption as "to be validated.")

3 · Where pipeline comes from — the shift the plan itself calls for

The territory: 340 France accounts in the database, 80 of them Tier 1-2 after scoring, 40 already addressed. Demos come from the whole machine, not only the 80: partners bring their own accounts (Banco de Chile came through the channel). The plan's own source mix, today → 2027:

Expansion (3 of the 19 S1 opps) sits outside this mix: it's the €1.1M line, owned by sales on existing accounts. And one honest arithmetic check: at S1's €31K of pipeline per demo, €2.1M a quarter would mean ~68 demos a quarter. In category creation, on this territory, that is not a number anyone should promise. The prudent read: the plan only balances if deals get bigger and conversion improves, not just if volume grows. That's what the outbound bet needs marketing for: a sequence that lands on an account that has already seen Actionable two or three times converts differently than 11%.

4 · The LinkedIn ABM program — the heavy lift, how it actually runs

Built and pressure-tested with a LinkedIn-ABM specialist who runs these programs on enterprise accounts, and who is lined up for the execution. His rules, not theory:

RuleHow it works
Budget shapeStart at €7.5K/month of media, not 10. €5K on the 80 in 3-4 clusters of 15-25 accounts grouped by use case; €2.5K on a wider same-profile audience that installs the category and tests messages at a 2-3x cheaper CPM before they run on the named list
Ads wear a faceThought Leader Ads from Nicolas and 1-2 internal experts, not the company page: ~2-3x the engagement. Category creation sells a problem, and a problem needs a person saying it
No 1:1 mediaLinkedIn won't serve ads to audiences under 300 people, so a single account can't be targeted. The top 10-15 accounts get named outbound, dedicated content and event invitations instead
Creative is the bottleneck3-5 fresh angles per month, budgeted as production separate from media. Without it the audience burns out in ~6 weeks
Events × media3-4 weeks before: target the attending accounts. During: short formats. After: retarget everyone touched, reach the target accounts that didn't come. Media multiplies the channel that already delivers, instead of competing with it
What it produces3-6 demos in the first full quarter, then 5-9 per quarter. Over a year, a first meeting with 25-35% of an 80-account list (20-28 meetings) is already top-tier ABM. Direct "saw the ad, booked a demo": 0-2 per quarter, and that's normal in category creation. The value is assisted demos
The 48h ruleWhen an account engages (clicks, video views), someone in sales acts within 48h. Without that owner, output falls to 1-2 demos per quarter and no media budget compensates. Decided before launch, with the written definition of "demo booked"
The rampMonths 1-2: setup and first signals. Months 3-4: first honestly attributable demos. With a 6-month sales cycle, the first ABM-attributable signature lands ~Q2 2027. Sept-Dec builds pipeline, not revenue

The economics. At cruising speed the program costs ~€90K a year of media. One signed client at Actionable's average ACV (~€100K, recurring) pays for the entire year of the ABM program by itself, and renews. The Sept-Dec slice costs €25-35K and produces the pipeline that signs in Q1-Q2 2027. A demo count gets debated every month. This equation doesn't.

5 · The website and the brand — nobody books through it, everybody checks it

No enterprise buyer fills a demo form. But every warmed account visits the site before replying to a rep, and every LLM a buyer asks pulls from what's published. Benchmarks for an established B2B SaaS site: 1.4-2.1% of visitors identify themselves (10,000 visits a year → 140-210 identified contacts feeding the touch machine). Today actionable.live is 3 pages with its strongest proof (Carrefour ×7 ROI, OUIGO +€390K) locked on one of them, and nobody searches the category yet. The 2027 inbound engine starts with the page that names the category and proof pages worth citing; until search exists, paid search covers brand and competitor terms only.

And what a warmed account finds has to look like it was built for them. Today the brand speaks to data engineers: a dark cockpit aesthetic, a hero line that files Actionable in the crowded AI-analytics shelf, and the category it claims nowhere in sight. The buyers are CMOs, CX directors and customer-knowledge teams. That mismatch between the story and the look is why the rebrand + new site sits in the 2027 budget below, chosen through a proper agency shortlist: not cosmetics, but making the category claim believable to the people who sign.
