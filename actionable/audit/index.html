02 · In your words

Your framing, in your own words.
If this reads right, the rest follows.

"Avec Actionable, je peux enfin agir proactivement, plutôt que constater a posteriori ce qui s'est passé."The customer sentence, in your own words

The goal

Enterprise pipeline generated inbound: prospects arriving already knowing the category. "We need Predictive CX, and we think Actionable is the leader."

The buying committee

CX / Customer Insights teams champion. The C-suite signs: the CMO at Cdiscount, Customer Strategy with Data at Edenred. The deal climbs from one room to the other over 5 to 6 months.

The real enemy

Deals rarely die against a named competitor. They die against the status quo: surveys, the Data queue, "not now". In buyers' words: "dès qu'on veut aller plus loin, on dépend de la Data et ça prend des semaines."

The machine today

Marketing is founder-led with one apprentice. Events cost €8-15k for 1-3 immediate leads each. Partners (Ipsos, Kantar, Havas, Capgemini, Vertone) bring 30% of opportunities: the strongest under-invested channel.

The pitch that wins the room

Surveys reach 2 to 5% of customers and get challenged on representativity. Actionable reads 100% of customers, individually, at 80%+ reliability, industrialised at Edenred and Cdiscount scale.

After the click, it works

05 · Brand, look & feel

The brand is dressed for the Data team.
Your champion is the CX leader.

The identity is a retro control room: terminal typography, CRT glitch, "// CAUSAL AI PREDICTION PLATFORM". Distinctive and technically credible, real assets. But the champion is a customer experience professional, empathy at scale as a job, greeted with surveillance aesthetics and "act before it's too late". The strongest Predictive CX brand would feel the way the product makes a CX leader feel: finally seeing their customers clearly. Human, warm, confident, technical depth one layer down.

A domain that invokes trust

You ask banks and retailers for their customer data; .live reads entertainment, not AI SaaS. Checked August 11: getactionable.tech is available cheap; actionable.ai and actionable.io are six figures and worth it. The domain only gets harder to change.

Start from the film

The film already tells it right: positive urgency, human, "satisfaction is no longer a mystery, it's a science", and a tagline-grade closer, "Prédire la satisfaction, décider l'action." The look around it is generic, which is normal: category creators get to set the look everyone else copies. That part is still to build.

The recommendation is a rebrand, not a repaint. The current identity was built from the inside (what we find cool) rather than from the champion (what makes them feel seen). And it isn't an invention job; it's an alignment job: the film already found the voice.

05 · The positioning, in their own words

Three names for one category,
written for the wrong reader.

Three labels at once

The homepage runs "Causal AI prediction platform" (kicker), "The Predictive Customer Experience AI" (headline), and Predictive CX (the category the company says it is creating). A category-creation strategy cannot afford three names. Pick one, make every surface repeat it.

Aimed at the wrong reader

"AI your data has been waiting for", "understand why your KPIs move", +15B transactions analysed: written for the Data team, the very status quo the product displaces. The single best line on the page, "Score 100% of your customer base, not a 5% survey sample", the exact wedge customers themselves describe, sits buried in module three.

Fear where delight should be

"Act before it's too late" (twice), "before they break": the founder's own internal language, and it is honest. But a CX leader lives in positive-sum language. Same urgency, flipped: act while you can still change the outcome. Delight, don't dread.

The name is the story, untold: customers describe getting a list each morning of clients at risk, and acting before anything breaks. "Actionable" is that promise. It appears nowhere on the website.
05 · The sales materials

Four decks, four framings.
The category lives in one of them.

AFRC one-pager · keep Generic sales deck · rework Predictive CX event deck · make it the master Investor pitch · retire from sales

Four framings for one company

"Prédire la satisfaction client grâce à l'IA" (AFRC), a "Predictive era" of CRM (generic deck), "Predictive CX" (the September event deck), "causal AI platform" (the investor deck, where the homepage's kicker comes from). Only the event deck builds the category the company says it is creating.

The best line is already written

Carrefour: 5% respondents, 82% of the base predicted, 80% reliability, next to x7 incremental revenue (generic deck). Category, wedge and blue-chip proof in one breath, sitting on slide 23 of 24. Promote it to slide 1 of everything.

Unproofed decks are a trust leak

"Template" still visible on four slides of the September deck, a broken hero sentence in the generic deck, French bleeding into EN slides. A vendor selling precision and reliability ships typos in its hero claims. Proofing is positioning.

And the committee's second room is unserved: no deck speaks the signer's language, brand, growth and budget defense for the C-suite. CX champions get served, data teams get served, the signature gets case-study ROI at best.

06 · The G2 play

G2 is a shortlist and a source.
That makes it strategic.

One profile feeds two audiences at once: enterprise buyers building shortlists, and the LLMs that lean on G2 when they answer category questions. Actionable sits in neither today.

Pick the right shelf

"Experience Management Software" is where Medallia and Qualtrics sit: that is the conversation to enter. "Feedback Analytics" is survey land (SurveyMonkey). Steer clear of survey-automation and AI-for-surveys categories: they are the opposite of the product.

It counts twice

learn.g2.com is already among the top-cited sources behind your category prompts (14 citations in the Trakkr baseline), and G2's weight has only grown since it absorbed its main rivals. A G2 profile is enterprise proof and AI visibility in one move.

The reviews already exist

Six enterprise clients, ~15 delivered logos. Reviews from Carrefour, OUIGO and Audiens-level names are proof listicle writers cannot fake, and competitors are already writing the listicles: Perspective AI publishes its own "10 AI CX platforms compared".

08 · Competitive review

Nobody owns predictive + CX together.
Yet.

Your watchlist, verified at source, splits into two games. Qualtrics and Medallia sell to CX buyers today. Prior Labs and Fundamental sell the predictive model layer to data teams: SAP bought Prior Labs in July 2026, Fundamental has raised $255M. Plus the real enemy: in-house Data teams and the status quo. "We have no competitors" is true at the product level; every one of these competes for the same budget line.

The label survives validation

CX Today publishes "Predictive CX AI Platforms 2026" rankings and CX Network runs a Predictive CX topic hub, yet no vendor claims the term, and no US startup runs it as their H1. Analysts would file Actionable under Voice of the Customer today. The shelf exists and is empty.

The brand map has an empty corner

Prior Labs, Fundamental and Hex dress for data scientists; Qualtrics dresses warm for the survey world it owns. Warm + predictive is the empty corner (see the brand map in 05). The first brand there takes the champion by default.

The window is short

The predictive layer is being armed and absorbed by the giants whose suites CX leaders already run, and CX Today reports providers "jostling" for labels in this space. Publish the category definition first, with the qualifier only Actionable can claim: 100% of customers, zero surveys.

08 · What their ads say

Everyone advertises in the dark.
The warm corner costs nothing to enter.

Fear in an engineer's costume

Medallia is the heaviest advertiser in the set: messages written for CX leaders ("CX leaders are under pressure", "Nice to have. Necessary to thrive.") dressed in black chrome and neon lime borrowed from dev tooling. The empathy position argued in the least empathetic visual language on LinkedIn.

One warm ad in twenty-five

Across four active advertisers, exactly one creative is warm, human and light-ground: Medallia's Shipt customer story. Fundamental writes only for "enterprise AI teams", Hex has one ad live in Europe, Prior Labs runs none. In France, the warm-human position is empty.

Even the empathy leaders slip

Two Medallia thought-leader ads render as "Hi %FIRSTNAME%,". A CX platform shipping a broken merge tag in paid media is the gap between claiming empathy and practicing it, on camera.

09 · The synthesis

Every yes here is real.
Every but stands between you and winning.

Real enterprise proof: Edenred, Cdiscount, millions of customers scored, 80%+ reliability.
All of it is squeezed into one unrankable page. A buyer who does not scroll, and 85% leave in 41 seconds, never meets the proof.
A distinctive new brand that nobody forgets.
It speaks to data engineers. Your champions are CX and insights people: empathy professionals walking into a control room.
A category being created: Predictive CX.
The category exists only inside your calls. Search demand for it is zero, and you own no surface to build it on.
Traffic multiplied by 22 since the May relaunch.
85% of it bounces in 41 seconds. There is one page to see and one thing to do: request a demo.
A clear champion motion and a proven sales narrative.
One bottom-funnel CTA serves a 5-to-6-month cycle. The curious CX manager who isn't demo-ready has nowhere to go, and no way to be seen again.
Partners already bring 30% of opportunities: Ipsos, Kantar, Havas, Capgemini, Vertone.
The joint "Predictive CX" offer exists, inside the partner portal. Publicly: zero commitment, no page, no press release, and Ipsos's logo leads the one-pager. The channel is rented, not owned.
10 · The strategic conclusion

One story, two rooms.
Everything above points here.

The story, told for the right person

Prediction puts CX at the center of the company: satisfaction stops being the soft metric and starts driving revenue, pricing and operations (the OUIGO conversation already proves it). A story a CX leader sees their future in, sharpened for the ICP, and defensible up to the C-suite.

The story, amplified

A proof and content layer Google can rank, LLMs can cite and journalists can quote: case studies as real pages, a category hub, PR and media presence. When a CX director googles you after an event or asks ChatGPT about the category, the answer is you.

A path for the not-ready-yet

Most of the market doesn't know this category exists, let alone that they need it. While they discover, they need journeys: something to read, keep, and send the boss. "Request a demo" is the last step, not the first.

Where we start: the first 90 days.

1 · Rebuild and dress the story

Positioning locked, then a rebrand to win: a challenger can get away with an okay brand; a category leader has to look the part, especially for enterprise.

2 · Build the foundation

A real website: proof pages, category hub, blog with a content engine behind it, and PR for the media presence you asked for on day one.

3 · Show up every day

Always-on between events: LinkedIn ABM on the 400 accounts, Nicolas's voice leading, paid support behind it.

11 · The action plan

Here is what I recommend we do,
in this order.

  1. Lock the story

    Positioning and messaging for the CX champion and the C-suite signer, plus the brand-warmth layer that makes the champion feel at home. Foundation for everything below.

  2. Put the proof back

    Case studies (Edenred, Cdiscount) restored and rebuilt as citable pages. The 10-to-1 ratio of case-study opens to demo clicks says buyers want proof before conversation.

  3. Build the ABM machine

    400 nameable target accounts. LinkedIn paid to named CMO/CX personas, founder voice (and other strong profiles) publishing consistently, events activated before, during and after, and the partner channel (already 30% of deals) systematised: a joint press release or a partner page pointing at actionable.live, with Ipsos first and then every partner. No generic inbound chase.

  4. Be the answer: SEO, GEO, PR

    A category hub and answer-shaped pages for Google and the LLMs, plus press and media presence. Validated category name, EN-first for the UK/US ambition, FR site for the home advantage. The goal: be the answer when the 400 accounts go looking.

  5. Middle-funnel paths + measurement

    CTAs for every stage of a 5-6 month cycle, capture that feeds the CRM, and funnel events you can actually see. A named, context-capturing demo journey instead of a bare Calendly.

11 · The first 90 days, concretely

The rebrand takes months.
Nothing else waits for it.

Days 1-30 · Foundations

Positioning and category name locked, messaging matched to the ICP. Quick brand cleanup on the current site (the cockpit goes, the brand stays). G2 profile live. Nicolas's LinkedIn rhythm starts. Rebrand agency brief out.

Days 31-60 · The engine

Proof pages and category hub live on the cleaned site, content machine publishing, PR groundwork, LinkedIn ABM on the 400 accounts, partner pages with Ipsos negotiated. Rebrand in build.

Days 61-90 · The splash

Rebrand lands on the new domain with a PR moment and the category definition published. Fast track: live in October. More runway if we want it: January, while the cleaned brand holds the line.

Two tracks the whole way: the rebrand in build, and the machine already running on the current brand. Nothing waits.
