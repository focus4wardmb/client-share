The wedge

Surveys reach 2 to 5% of customers and get challenged on representativity. Actionable reads 100% of customers, individually, at 80%+ reliability, industrialised at Edenred and Cdiscount scale.

The proof still exists, and it is strong: Carrefour (x7 incremental ROI, two Cas d'Or awards), OUIGO/SNCF (+390k€ incremental revenue in 6 months), with video success stories. But it lives as sections of a single page: Google cannot rank it, LLMs cannot cite it, and a champion cannot send their CMO a link to it. Referral mix: Google 84% (almost all branded navigation), VivaTech, LinkedIn, and your investor's own site. Notably, chatgpt.com already appears as a referrer: buyers are asking AI tools about you before you have anything for AI tools to read.

Tested, and worth saying: the machine after the click works. A demo request gets an instant confirmation and a personal email from the head of sales. The problem is not what happens after the click. It is that almost nobody clicks, and everyone who does not click is invisible.

Brand, look & feel

Who the brand talks to today, versus who buys.

The current identity is a retro control room: terminal typography, CRT glitch, dark cockpit imagery, "// CAUSAL AI PREDICTION PLATFORM". It is distinctive and technically credible, and those are real assets. But look at it through the eyes of the buying committee: your champion is a customer experience professional, someone whose job is empathy at scale, and the site greets them with surveillance-room aesthetics and "act before it's too late". The brand is dressed for the Data team, and the Data team is the status quo you displace, not the champion who carries you upstairs.

AI does not have to look like a machine. The strongest Predictive CX brand would feel the way the product makes a CX leader feel: finally seeing their customers clearly. Human, warm, confident, with the technical depth one layer down for the validation conversation.

The recommendation is a rebrand, not a repaint. The current identity is striking, but it does not stand for anything a CX leader can attach values to: it was built from the inside (what we find cool) rather than from the champion (what makes them feel seen). CX buyers choose vendors the way they run their own function: on empathy, clarity and trust signals.

The domain is part of the brand problem. actionable.live reads like a streaming site, not an enterprise vendor. For a company asking national banks for their customer data, actionable.com, or at minimum actionable.ai, is a trust signal worth buying. It also matters for search: today Google mostly meets this brand through its legal pages.

The best brand asset already exists, and Actionable made it: the brand film. Aspirational, positive urgency, human — "satisfaction is no longer a mystery, it's a science", "we make the silence speak", and a tagline-grade closer: "Prédire la satisfaction, décider l'action." The website simply doesn't keep the promise the film makes. The rebrand isn't an invention job; it's an alignment job.

The positioning, in their own words

The homepage currently runs three different category labels at once: "Causal AI prediction platform" (the kicker), "The Predictive Customer Experience AI" (the headline), and Predictive CX (the category the company says it is creating). A category-creation strategy cannot afford three names. Pick one, and make every surface repeat it.

The language is aimed at the wrong reader. "AI your data has been waiting for", "understand why your KPIs move", +15B transactions analysed: this is written for the Data team, the very status quo the product displaces. Meanwhile the single best line on the page, "Score 100% of your customer base, not a 5% survey sample", the exact wedge customers themselves describe, sits buried in module three.

And the urgency is framed as fear: "act before it's too late" (twice), "before they break". Notably, that phrasing is the founder's own internal language. It is honest, but a CX leader lives in positive-sum language. Same urgency, flipped: act while you can still change the outcome. Delight, don't dread.

Competitive review

Who occupies the space around "Predictive CX" today, and what they own.

Nicolas's own watchlist: Qualtrics and Medallia (voice of customer), Priorlabs and Fundamental (predictive), Hex (AI analytics) — plus the real enemy, in-house Data teams, for whom Dataiku ships ready-made churn-prediction solution kits. "We have no competitors" is true only at the product level; at the budget level, all of these compete for the same line.

The category namespace is contested at the edges: Medallia calls itself an "enterprise experience platform", Qualtrics owns "experience management". The survey incumbents own the word "experience"; nobody yet owns "predictive" + "CX" together. That is the opening — if the label survives tomorrow's LLM and search validation.

And the brand map has an empty corner. Priorlabs and Hex dress for data scientists (dense, technical). Fundamental is the most memorable brand of the set — bold, distinct — but still AI-coded. Qualtrics is friendlier but flat and interchangeable. Every AI player defaults to dark-plus-one-flashy-color, including Actionable itself. Nobody in the space dresses for the CX leader. The first brand that does takes the champion by default.

One story, two rooms

The strategic conclusion everything above points to.

Tighter story

One narrative that a CX manager can fall in love with and a CMO can defend to a CFO. Today the story lives in your calls and nowhere else. It needs to be written, warmed up, and put where buyers are before the call.

Visible story

A proof and content layer that Google can rank and LLMs can cite: case studies as real pages, a category hub, answer-shaped content. Not to chase inbound leads (those skew SMB) — as air cover for the enterprise motion: when a CX director googles you after an event or asks ChatGPT about the category, the answer is you.

A path for the not-yet-ready

A 5-to-6-month cycle needs middle steps: something to read, something to keep, something to send the boss. "Request a demo" is the last step of the journey, not the only one.

10

Here is what I recommend focusing on. This list is by order of priority.

  1. The story, told for two rooms

    Positioning and messaging for the CX champion and the CMO signer, plus the brand-warmth layer that makes the champion feel at home. Foundation for everything below.

  2. Put the proof back

    Case studies (Edenred, Cdiscount) restored and rebuilt as citable pages. The 10-to-1 ratio of case-study opens to demo clicks says buyers want proof before conversation.

  3. Build the ABM machine

    400 nameable target accounts. LinkedIn paid to named CMO/CX personas, founder voice (and other strong profiles) publishing consistently, events activated before, during and after, and the partner channel (already 30% of deals) systematised. No generic inbound chase.

  4. Category air cover: SEO + GEO

    A category hub and answer-shaped pages built for Google and the LLMs — validated category name, EN-first for the UK/US ambition. The goal is not lead capture; it is being the answer when the 400 accounts go looking.

  5. Middle-funnel paths + measurement

    CTAs for every stage of a 5-6 month cycle, capture that feeds the CRM, and funnel events you can actually see. A named, context-capturing demo journey instead of a bare Calendly.
