02 · What Nicolas told us

Your framing, in your own words. If this reads right, the rest follows.

The goal

A meaningful share of enterprise pipeline generated inbound, with prospects arriving already knowing Actionable and the category: "We need Predictive CX, and we think Actionable is the leader."

The buying committee

CX / Customer Insights teams champion. The CMO signs. Two different conversations, and the deal climbs from one to the other over 5 to 6 months.

The real enemy

Deals almost never die against a named competitor. They die against the status quo: surveys, internal Data teams, "not now".

The machine today

Marketing is founder-led (~50% of lead gen) with one apprentice. Each event or award costs €8-15k and returns 1-3 immediate leads. Partners (Ipsos, Kantar, Havas, Capgemini, Vertone) already bring 30% of opportunities: the strongest under-invested channel.

The wedge

Surveys reach 2 to 5% of customers and get challenged on representativity. Actionable reads 100% of customers, individually, at 80%+ reliability, industrialised at Edenred and Cdiscount scale.

06 · The G2 play

G2 is a shortlist and a source. Actionable sits in neither.

One profile feeds two audiences at once: enterprise buyers building shortlists, and the LLMs that lean on G2 when they answer category questions.

Pick the right shelf

"Experience Management Software" is where Medallia and Qualtrics sit: that is the conversation to enter. "Feedback Analytics" is survey land (SurveyMonkey). Steer clear of survey-automation and AI-for-surveys categories: they are the opposite of the product.

It counts twice

learn.g2.com is already among the top-cited sources behind your category prompts (14 citations in the Trakkr baseline). A G2 profile is enterprise proof and AI visibility in one move.

The reviews already exist

Six enterprise clients, ~15 delivered logos. Reviews from Carrefour, OUIGO and Audiens-level names are proof listicle writers cannot fake, and competitors are already writing the listicles: Perspective AI publishes its own "10 AI CX platforms compared".

07 · Brand, look & feel

The brand is dressed for the Data team. The Data team is your status quo, not your champion.

The identity is a retro control room: terminal typography, CRT glitch, "// CAUSAL AI PREDICTION PLATFORM". Distinctive and technically credible, real assets. But the champion is a customer experience professional, empathy at scale as a job, greeted with surveillance aesthetics and "act before it's too late". The strongest Predictive CX brand would feel the way the product makes a CX leader feel: finally seeing their customers clearly. Human, warm, confident, technical depth one layer down.

The domain is part of the problem

actionable.live reads like a streaming site, not an enterprise vendor. Checked August 11: getactionable.tech is available for a low price; actionable.ai and actionable.io are both available in the six figures. For a company asking national banks for their customer data, the premium domain is worth it: it is the one thing that gets harder to change every month.

The best brand asset already exists

The brand film. Aspirational, positive urgency, human: "satisfaction is no longer a mystery, it's a science", "we make the silence speak", and a tagline-grade closer: "Prédire la satisfaction, décider l'action." The website simply doesn't keep the promise the film makes.

The recommendation is a rebrand, not a repaint. The current identity was built from the inside (what we find cool) rather than from the champion (what makes them feel seen). And it isn't an invention job; it's an alignment job: the film already found the voice.

07 · The positioning, in their own words

Three names for one category, written for the wrong reader.

Three labels at once

The homepage runs "Causal AI prediction platform" (kicker), "The Predictive Customer Experience AI" (headline), and Predictive CX (the category the company says it is creating). A category-creation strategy cannot afford three names. Pick one, make every surface repeat it.

Aimed at the wrong reader

"AI your data has been waiting for", "understand why your KPIs move", +15B transactions analysed: written for the Data team, the very status quo the product displaces. The single best line on the page, "Score 100% of your customer base, not a 5% survey sample", the exact wedge customers themselves describe, sits buried in module three.

Fear where delight should be

"Act before it's too late" (twice), "before they break": the founder's own internal language, and it is honest. But a CX leader lives in positive-sum language. Same urgency, flipped: act while you can still change the outcome. Delight, don't dread.

08 · Competitive review

Nobody owns "predictive" + "CX" together. Yet.

Your watchlist, verified at source, splits into two games. Qualtrics and Medallia sell to CX buyers today. Prior Labs and Fundamental sell the predictive model layer to data teams: SAP bought Prior Labs in July 2026, Fundamental has raised $255M. Plus the real enemy: in-house Data teams and the status quo. "We have no competitors" is true at the product level; every one of these competes for the same budget line.

The label survives validation

CX Today publishes "Predictive CX AI Platforms 2026" rankings and CX Network runs a Predictive CX topic hub, yet no vendor claims the term, and no US startup runs it as their H1. Analysts would file Actionable under Voice of the Customer today. The shelf exists and is empty.

The brand map has an empty corner

Prior Labs, Fundamental and Hex dress for data scientists; Qualtrics dresses warm for the survey world it owns. Warm + predictive is the empty corner (see the brand map in 07). The first brand there takes the champion by default.

The window is short

The predictive layer is being armed and absorbed by the giants whose suites CX leaders already run, and CX Today reports providers "jostling" for labels in this space. Publish the category definition first, with the qualifier only Actionable can claim: 100% of customers, zero surveys.

08 · What their ads say

Everyone advertises in the dark. The warm corner costs nothing to enter.

Medallia is the heaviest advertiser in the set: messages written for CX leaders ("CX leaders are under pressure", "Nice to have. Necessary to thrive.") dressed in black chrome and neon lime borrowed from dev tooling. The empathy position argued in the least empathetic visual language on LinkedIn.

One warm ad in twenty-five

Across four active advertisers, exactly one creative is warm, human and light-ground: Medallia's Shipt customer story. Fundamental writes only for "enterprise AI teams", Hex has one ad live in Europe, Prior Labs runs none. In France, the warm-human position is empty.

Even the empathy leaders slip

09 · The strategic conclusion

One story, two rooms. Everything above points here.

Tighter story

One narrative that a CX manager can fall in love with and a CMO can defend to a CFO. Today the story lives in your calls and nowhere else. It needs to be written, warmed up, and put where buyers are before the call.

Visible story

A proof and content layer that Google can rank and LLMs can cite: case studies as real pages, a category hub, answer-shaped content. Not to chase inbound leads (those skew SMB): air cover for the enterprise motion. When a CX director googles you after an event or asks ChatGPT about the category, the answer is you.

A path for the not-yet-ready

A 5-to-6-month cycle needs middle steps: something to read, something to keep, something to send the boss. "Request a demo" is the last step of the journey, not the only one.

10 · Priorities

Here is what I recommend focusing on. This list is by order of priority.

  1. The story, told for two rooms

    Positioning and messaging for the CX champion and the CMO signer, plus the brand-warmth layer that makes the champion feel at home. Foundation for everything below.

  2. Put the proof back

    Case studies (Edenred, Cdiscount) restored and rebuilt as citable pages. The 10-to-1 ratio of case-study opens to demo clicks says buyers want proof before conversation.

  3. Build the ABM machine

    400 nameable target accounts. LinkedIn paid to named CMO/CX personas, founder voice (and other strong profiles) publishing consistently, events activated before, during and after, and the partner channel (already 30% of deals) systematised. No generic inbound chase.

  4. Category air cover: SEO + GEO

    A category hub and answer-shaped pages built for Google and the LLMs: validated category name, EN-first for the UK/US ambition. The goal is not lead capture; it is being the answer when the 400 accounts go looking.

  5. Middle-funnel paths + measurement

    CTAs for every stage of a 5-6 month cycle, capture that feeds the CRM, and funnel events you can actually see. A named, context-capturing demo journey instead of a bare Calendly.
