Marketing audit · Written synthesis · August 12, 2026

Actionable: the category is ours to create.
The engine to claim it doesn't exist yet.

Prepared by Miri Blum · Focus4ward · for Nicolas Rieul, Co-Founder & Co-CEO, Actionable · Full presentation: clients.focus4ward.co/actionable/audit/ · Sources: Umami (90 days), Google Search Console (3 months + coverage), Trakkr AI-visibility baseline, SEMrush, live LLM captures (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini), 4 sales decks, 25 competitor ad captures, competitor sites verified at source, Ipsos partner portal, LinkedIn public audit, discovery interview and written answers.

The finding

Actionable is creating a category on calls, with nowhere for that category to live between the calls. Enterprise buyers don't search; they shortlist. Getting on the shortlist takes visibility where they look, and trust built along the whole 5-to-6-month cycle.

The business is real: €1.6M ARR, 6 active enterprise clients, ~15 large logos delivered (Carrefour, SNCF/OUIGO, Sephora, ENGIE, Fnac Darty, Audiens), 10-25% first-meeting-to-signature on ICP. Acquisition is founder-carried (50% events, 30% partners, 20% network) with near-zero inbound. Nothing is broken; nothing is plugged in.

What the audit found

  • Website & funnel: 2,140 visitors in 90 days, 92% of views on the homepage, 1.2 pages per visit, 85% bounce in 41 seconds, 1 demo click. The proof lives as sections of one unrankable page, and Google still holds 10 dead URLs returning 404. After the click, the machine works; almost nobody clicks.
  • Organic search: 735 clicks on 35,700 impressions, 100% from brand terms. US CTR 0.37% vs France 22.8%, and the site speaks only English. Google indexes 6 of 35 known URLs; 10 of the skipped are relaunch ghosts returning 404; the sitemap declares 3 pages. SEMrush registers Authority Score 23 and 255 referring domains pointing at a site where only 2 pages earn traffic.
  • Brand & positioning: the identity is dressed for the Data team; the champion is a CX leader. Three category labels run on the homepage, a fourth and fifth across the decks; the strongest wedge line sits buried in module three; urgency is framed as fear where a CX buyer lives in positive-sum language. The brand film already tells the story right. The domain (.live) works against enterprise trust; actionable.ai/.io are available at a price worth paying.
  • Sales materials: four decks, four framings; only the September Ipsos event deck builds "Predictive CX". Best line in the arsenal: Carrefour, 5% respondents / 82% of the base predicted / 80% reliability, next to x7 incremental revenue. Unproofed decks ("Template" placeholders, broken hero sentences) leak trust for a precision vendor.
  • AI visibility: 0 of 23 buyer-intent prompts name Actionable (1 mention across 1,845 source citations vs 151 competitor mentions). Live checks on ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini match: asked by name, all three answer well; asked as a buyer, none names Actionable. Perspective AI (conversational forms, no prediction) wins the survey-alternatives answers through its own content.
  • G2: a shortlist for enterprise buyers and a top-cited LLM source (14 citations in the baseline); Actionable sits in neither. Enter "Experience Management Software" where Medallia and Qualtrics sit; steer clear of survey-automation categories.
  • LinkedIn: Nicolas (29,000 followers) is 12x the company page (2,460) at one post a week, posting milestones rather than category opinion. Qualtrics: 332k; Perspective AI: 766. The one channel already winnable with cadence alone.
  • Competition: the watchlist splits into CX-product players (Qualtrics, Medallia) and model-layer players (Prior Labs, acquired by SAP in July 2026; Fundamental, $255M raised). "Predictive CX" is validated as a category name: CX Today ranks "Predictive CX AI Platforms 2026", CX Network runs a topic hub, and no vendor claims the term. In 25 ad-library captures, every LinkedIn creative is dark-ground; the warm-human position is empty. The window is short: publish the category definition first.
  • Partners: Ipsos brings deals and the joint "Predictive CX" offer exists inside the partner portal, but publicly Ipsos has zero commitment (no page, no press release) and its logo leads the shared one-pager. The channel is rented, not owned: negotiate the public pointer (press release or partner page linking to actionable.live), with Ipsos first and every partner after.

The strategy

One story, two rooms. Prediction puts CX at the center of the company: satisfaction stops being the soft metric and starts driving revenue, pricing and operations. The story is told for the right person (the CX champion, sharpened for the ICP, defensible up to the C-suite), amplified where buyers look (search, AI answers, G2, LinkedIn, PR), with journeys for the not-ready-yet, because most of this market doesn't yet know the category exists.

The action plan, in order

  • 1 · Lock the story: positioning and messaging for champion and signer, plus the brand-warmth layer. Foundation for everything below.
  • 2 · Put the proof back: case studies restored as citable pages; buyers open proof 10x more than they click demos.
  • 3 · Build the ABM machine: 400 named accounts, LinkedIn with Nicolas's voice leading, events activated before/during/after, the partner channel made public starting with Ipsos.
  • 4 · Be the answer: SEO, GEO and PR: category hub, answer-shaped pages, media presence; EN-first for UK/US, FR site for the home advantage.
  • 5 · Middle-funnel paths + measurement: CTAs for every stage of the cycle, capture that feeds the CRM, funnel events you can see.

30 / 60 / 90

Days 1-30: positioning and category name locked, messaging matched to the ICP, quick brand cleanup on the current site, G2 profile live, Nicolas's LinkedIn rhythm starts, rebrand brief out.

Days 31-60: proof pages and category hub live, content machine publishing, PR groundwork, LinkedIn ABM running, Ipsos public pointer negotiated, rebrand in build.

Days 61-90: rebrand lands on the new domain with a PR moment and the published category definition. Fast track: October. More runway: January, while the cleaned brand holds the line.

Success criteria

Category searches exist and grow · AI engines name Actionable on buyer questions (Trakkr tracked monthly) · G2 profile live with enterprise reviews · LinkedIn engagement on category content · inbound enterprise conversations arriving already knowing the category.
