Pennylane vs Qonto and Spendesk

A full read on Qonto and Spendesk, company, positioning, product, reputation and presence, and where the opening is for Pennylane after the January 2026 Series F.

Prepared by Focus4ward · May 2026 · Public-source figures, sourced inline, verify before external use · Confidential
Executive summary

Pennylane sits in the French SME finance stack alongside two well-funded neighbours, but they do not sell the same thing. Qonto is the business bank account, regulated by the ACPR, with 600,000+ clients across eight European markets, €448.7M in 2024 revenue, and roughly 171,000 LinkedIn followers. Spendesk is CFO-side spend control for scaling companies, with the deepest review base of the three (~413 G2 reviews at 4.6/5). Pennylane is the accounting layer with a bank account and cards bolted on, and the only one of the three with an accountant distribution channel: ~6,000 firms processing books for 800,000+ client companies. After the €175M January 2026 round at a $4.25B valuation, the opening is to lock that channel as a moat before Qonto or Spendesk attempt to reach upstream into accounting, and to use the German expansion, AI and payments budget to make the position unambiguous in market.

What the data says

Six reads on where Pennylane's opening is, and where the bar actually sits.

01

They are not the same product

Qonto is a regulated business bank account, Spendesk is CFO spend control, Pennylane is accounting + finance with a pro account on top. The "all-in-one finance" sentence hides three different sells. Pennylane's positioning has to make the accounting-first wedge unambiguous, otherwise it gets compared on banking against Qonto and on spend against Spendesk and loses both.

02

The accountant channel is the moat

6,000+ accounting firms route 800,000+ client companies through Pennylane. Neither Qonto nor Spendesk has anything similar. Once a firm closes its clients' books in Pennylane, the switching cost lives at the firm, not the SME. That is the durable asset the rest of the strategy should defend.

03

Qonto is the scale benchmark, not the model

Qonto is 2x Pennylane on headcount (~2,335 vs ~1,111) and 1.9x on LinkedIn (~171k vs ~91k), built over 9 years of pan-European banking. Trying to match that footprint is the wrong game. Pennylane is younger, more capital-efficient per round, and approaching profitability — different motion, different metrics.

04

Spendesk owns review density, not category

~413 G2 reviews at 4.6/5 vs Qonto's 154 and Pennylane's smaller pool. Spendesk's CFO buyer reviews software for a living, so the gap is the buyer, not the product. Pennylane will not match it through self-serve; it can match it through the accountant network surfacing reviews at the firm level.

05

Germany is the next contested market

The €175M Series F earmarks German expansion, generative AI and payments. Qonto acquired Penta in 2022 and is already operating in Germany; Spendesk has Datev integration. Pennylane's wedge in Germany is the same as in France — accountant-first — and the window is the next 12–18 months before Qonto reaches upstream into accounting.

06

The gaps in reviews tell the bar

"Still feature gaps in accounting" (mass matching, rule configuration) and Tier-1 support friction are the recurring complaints. The accounting depth is the exact thing Pennylane owns versus the others — turning that into a non-negotiable execution priority is what protects the channel. Position is set by the product the accountant trusts, not by the marketing on top.
