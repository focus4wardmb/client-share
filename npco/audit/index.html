The story is told at half size

Both founders already tell the engineer-centred story in the room: empowerment, out of the parametric prison. The public surface says "1000x faster" like everyone else. The positioning can be sharper, and pushed much further.

There is no funnel

03 · The buyer & the motion

Experts first. Then the top.
The middle signs, it doesn't decide.

The motion that wins: technical validation with the pure expert, then straight to the CTO with "your own expert validated this". Emmanuel's words: "on parle à la fois en bas et en haut". At Air Liquide the engineer next to Emmanuel called the executive committee of a €20B group when something was wrong. In French industrial groups, the expert IS the influencer.

The champion

The simulation engineer / technical expert. Falls in love on the benchmark, validates, escalates.

The signature

Program director → BU director (Safran pattern): the budget path that formalizes what the expert validated and the top endorsed. Ambition: comex-level strategic buys.

The cycle

6-12 months cold; much faster when already known in the group. Almost no RFPs: this is a need-creation market, which is precisely what marketing is for.

The champion must be influent, not just convinced. The known failure mode is winning the engineer while the information never reaches the budget holder. His value angle for the champion: he shines inside his organisation, designing products he could never have imagined. And the interface is about to change: application engineers now being recruited will become the main client-facing layer, and the champion playbook needs to survive that handoff.Matthieu, Aug 13 · confirms the motion independently, and sharpens it

PhysicsX: "Accelerating Industrial Innovation with AI". Emmi: "Large Engineering Models". Luminary: "Physics AI at scale." The convergence is total: technology as hero, speed as proof. "The optimal design already exists" has the same flaw: no one in the sentence designs anything.

The open seat

The engineer-centred story: escape the parametric prison, explore what you could never test, change what engineering dares to try. Empowerment (Emmanuel's word) against everyone else's automation story. Matthieu lands on the same word unprompted: "empowering the engineers", not replacing them; both founders already tell this story; only the public surface doesn't.

The trust pillar

Every buyer will ask "when do I trust the surrogate?" Matthieu's answer is already built into the product: a confidence index on every prediction, and a clean line: NPco doesn't replace certified simulation, it explores the design space upstream so you validate one final design instead of 50–100. Say it publicly, first, and own the conversation. NPco's own survey confirms the stakes: trust is the #3 adoption barrier (47%).

"Candidate altitude: faster and safer products, not faster engineers. More of the design space explored means better, cleaner, safer machines, sooner. 05 · Brand architecture

Four pillars hold this brand.
The site, the deck and LinkedIn still lead with speed.

Four pillars, each one true, each one provable. Speed stays: it's real, and buyers ask for it. But every player in the cohort says it too, so speed alone can't differentiate. The pillars are what the public story doesn't say yet.

1 · Empowerment

The engineer at the centre, out of the parametric prison. Emmanuel's own word. Nobody else in the cohort puts the person in the sentence.

2 · Dare to go beyond

Where "the optimal design already exists" goes next: the design you always dreamed of but never had the time or budget to simulate. Coupled with empowerment. Subtle in site copy, explicit in ads and campaigns, never forced into the headline.

3 · Trust in AI

The predictable objection, answered head-on: a confidence index on every prediction, and AI replacing the iterations, never the final verification. Certification unchanged. First to say this publicly owns the conversation.

4 · Yours, not rented

Trained on your simulations, run on your machines, and the model is yours, IP included. On-prem alone is now table stakes in the cohort; explicit model ownership is the claim nobody else makes publicly.

The reason to believe is the founders themselves: they came from simulation into AI, not the reverse. Emmanuel, ten years in simulation across academia and industry, PhD at Inria. Matthieu, applied mathematician, PhD Paris-Saclay/Inria, NeurIPS workshop 2022. Behind them: 98% on Safran's benchmark, the NeurIPS submission, Inria lineage. Trust pillar and proof, in two bios.

Finish the rebrand

Not three names by choice: an unfinished transition. AI engines still cite augursim.ai, the fundraising deck is fully Augur, the draft site mixes NP Co. / NP AI / NPCO.ai, and its T&C says "NP Co. SAS" where the legal entity is AUGUR SAS. Buyers and machines keep meeting two companies.

One domain, one slug

Everything on one domain: email, site, documents; redirect the rest. linkedin.com/company/npco belongs to another French company: worth asking LinkedIn whether the page is dormant and the name reclaimable. Perplexity resolves "NPCO" to a Saudi aviation firm and "Augur" to a robotics digital twin. Finishing the rebrand is also an AI-visibility fix.

One boilerplate

One company description everywhere: site, deck, LinkedIn, PR, papers. V1 to react to: "NPco builds AI physics models that let engineers explore the full design space at the speed of their ideas. Founded by Inria researchers, its models run on-premise, learn from your simulations, and let you validate one final design instead of fifty. Safran and EDF already engineer with NPco." V1 · validate at restitution

The pointThe pillars are already there, and each one is provable. What's left is saying them, everywhere, under one name. One open call for the founders: the flag. Emmanuel says drop French and Inria (US-first); Matthieu names European sovereignty as a differentiator. The practical shape is per-surface: a French site can carry sovereignty; the US-facing English site doesn't say it. Choose it, then build for it.
