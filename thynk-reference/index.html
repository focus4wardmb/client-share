01 — Context

Context and environment

The market is growing fast, dominated by giants, and fragmented at the same time. Thynk is a challenger entering a market that's only partly ready for what the product can do.

Market

  • The global Smart Hospitality market was valued at $13.7Bn in 2022 and is projected to reach $130.6Bn by 2030 (CAGR 29.5%).
  • The growing demand for guest-oriented, hyperconnected personalisation and real-time optimised experience will drive the smart hospitality market.
Sources: Verified Market Research · MarketsandMarkets

Thynk

  • The market is historically dominated by giants like Oracle and Amadeus.
  • Thynk is a challenger on a market that is partly (at least in Europe) unprepared for the full scope of the product's capabilities (and price).
  • The US and European markets differ greatly in terms of market maturity and GTM approach.

Competitors

  • The market is highly fragmented. It's divided into small parts (POS, PMS), managed by many different software providers.
  • Clients are looking to centralise operations, reduce manual labour, increase efficiency, and boost revenue.
  • They will opt for a solution that is cloud native, integrated, easy and fast to implement, and has great support.
02 — Methodology

Audit methodology

Three workstreams ran in parallel over a six-week engagement: tools and material assessment, structured interviews with leadership and team, and the synthesis into recommendations with ROI ranking.

Assessment

  • Website performance
  • SEO performance
  • Paid search performance
  • HubSpot configuration and usage
  • Brand materials
  • Product marketing materials
  • Marketing plan, KPIs, budget
  • Financial state
  • Campaigns and content
  • Competitors review
  • Sales materials

Interviews

  • Interviewing the marketing team.
  • Interviewing key stakeholders: CEO, Sales EU, Sales US, Product, CFO.
  • Reviewing additional Sales, Product and Finance materials.

Ideation

  • Understand what is needed to win (ideal world) based on the context and my past experience.
  • Gap analysis.
  • Understanding the causes of the gaps (dynamics).
  • Suggesting ways to overcome them.
  • Recommendations for improvements in the short, mid, and long term, with associated investment and ROI.
03 — Ideal-world pillars

Pillars of a marketing strategy

If we were building the function from scratch with no constraints, these are the five pillars it would rest on. Used as the gap-analysis frame in Phase 3.

Brand awareness and perception
Leveraging PR, AR and organic to increase reach and convey the relevant message about who we are and what is our unique, innovative solution for ENT and SMBs.
Lead generation and pipeline growth
Building an inbound machine: generating leads, enriching, scoring, and qualifying them, followed by either passing them to Sales or nurturing them.
Product marketing and positioning
Make the product stand out by positioning it as avant-garde. Crafting the appropriate packaging, messaging, and pricing to maximise revenue and upsell opportunities.
Sales enablement and outbound
Support sales efforts by creating stunning sales enablement materials that resonate with our target audience and are effective at the local market level. Execute on ABM campaigns.
Sales-Marketing-Product alignment
Enhance collaboration and synergy between Sales, Marketing, and Product by aligning objectives and roadmaps and establishing effective processes.
04 — Voice of the team

What I heard

Verbatim from the stakeholder interviews. The recurring pattern: trust, process, and data are the three things that aren't yet there. Each one was named separately by people who didn't talk to each other before the interviews.

We don't have a culture of A/B testing.
There is a difference in how we sell in the US compared to Europe: we don't use the same language and the pain-points are not even the same.
Our inbound strategy is low to non-existent. In the past 8 months I got 4 low quality leads.
People who know us still can't figure out what we do from our website.
If we don't have a process internally for Sales, Marketing and Ops, we'll lose deals.
There aren't any marketing materials, and the ones we have are not good, design and copy wise.
We change what we do and who we are every 3 weeks. When a startup changes their messaging over and over again, we cannot trust them.
We've shown Marketing we don't trust them.
There is a huge data gap. We have no visibility on lead status, I need to manually ask the people.
Our messaging doesn't resonate globally. We need to decentralise decisions.
We keep adding more bricks on bricks. We need to be more aligned internally with stakeholders.
We spend $200K a year on events. We need to do better before and after, and we need a better process for tradeshows.
One of the top prospects I've been talking to said: I've been on your website and I don't understand what you do.
05 — Review of current state

Pillar-by-pillar review

Six pillars covered: Website, Brand, Lead Generation, Product Marketing, Comms & Social, and Team / Process / Data. Each block surfaces observations, what to continue, what to stop or start, and a rough Investment / Impact / Time-to-results read.

AWebsite
Clean design, but text-heavy and not optimised for the buyer journey. Mobile and US localisation are the two biggest gaps.

Observations — UX, UI, content

  • Clean and modern design, yet generic and unmemorable.
  • The website is text-heavy, making it difficult for users to focus on the key message.
  • The client carousel is very good, but other proof points should be more prominent.
  • No pricing page (potential conversion lift, ref: Mews).
  • The website has a chatbot, but it is offline.
  • URLs still need improvement for SEO.
  • Success stories should be in the main navigation; solutions don't include case studies.
  • The website is not optimised for mobile (41.8% of sessions are mobile).
  • English only, while 67% of revenue comes from Europe and the US is not optimised.

Web performance + lead generation

  • About 5K users per month, with 95% being new users. Main geos: US, FR, DE, UK, India.
  • Traffic sources: Direct 57%, organic search 20%, referral 6%, organic social (LinkedIn) 8%, paid social 6%, other 3%.
  • Organic search generates more engaged sessions than direct traffic (41% vs 34%) and higher avg time (1m02) and events per session (7.01).
  • In January: 24 leads and 13 prospects (most from direct traffic). Booking calendar doesn't always work, no confirmation email.
  • The form is not designed to be visible above the fold; scoring and data enrichment not done.
Continue
  • Generate customer testimonials and feature them on the site.
Stop / Start
  • Reduce copy and polish, focus on key messages.
  • Improve design and platform shots; add UI moments and videos.
  • Improve mobile experience.
  • Test CTAs, copy, and layout.
  • Move key items to main navigation.
  • Fix SEO technical issues, fix URLs.
  • Consider adding a product tour or a demo.
  • Create a multi-region / multi-language site.
  • Allow non-work emails on contact form; remove company size and number of properties.

BBrand
A "next-generation" positioning that the brand identity doesn't yet live up to. Three missing elements: reassurance, momentum, and movement.

Positioning + messaging

  • Using "Next generation" is very good. Thynk is identified as a disruptor and fosters innovation.
  • While change is desired, it's not always easy. Three key elements are missing: reassurance, momentum, movement.
  • Reassurance: Thynk needs to be perceived as a trusted advisor. Proof points (business results, quotes) need to be strategically placed.
  • Movement: Thynk is uniquely positioned to drive customer-centric digitalisation in hospitality. The "bigger than us" opportunity is not fully reflected in the copy.
  • Momentum: Hoteliers need to feel the urgency: if they don't jump on the spacecraft, they'll be left behind.
  • For advanced users: everyone is talking about AI. Without data, AI is impossible.

Look & feel + tone of voice

  • Recently rebranded and therefore more "put together," yet not polished.
  • The brand doesn't convey high-end, market-leading, or world-class.
  • Discrepancy between "next-gen" and brand elements (like icons) that are archaic.
  • Slides are cluttered, suggesting a complex solution.
  • Branding is not always consistent or unique. Platform screenshots need improvement.
  • TOV is corporate and professional, but not memorable or unique.
  • Word choice matters: use "reshaped" or "transformed" instead of "converted"; "enable" and "empower" instead of "help."
Continue
  • Keep "next generation" as the core positioning angle.
  • Keep using action verbs that create dynamic rhythm.
Stop / Start
  • Restructure and refocus the brand narrative.
  • Recreate essential brand elements: mission, vision, USP, tagline.
  • Polish the copy using a professional copywriter.
  • Revise the brand design to make it more modern, crisp, and ENT-worthy.
  • Introduce captivating content: UI moments and product videos.
  • Create a brand book, TOV and style guide.

CLead generation
Branded keywords work; non-branded ranks have been flat for 16 months. Events absorb 74% of budget without process or ROI tracking.

General + SEO + paid search

  • Generated 23 leads (40% from search) in Jan and 18 leads in Feb (60% from search).
  • Affiliate content such as HotelTechReport is not present.
  • Content is often produced without a clear understanding of how it will be utilised, often without SEO in mind.
  • SEO is not treated as a priority (SEMrush or SpyFu not used).
  • Non-brand keywords have maintained low positions for the past 16 months.
  • Branded keywords show improvement in impressions, clicks, and CTRs.
  • Test campaign generated learning but lacked impact (only 3 leads): inadequate budget, not geo-optimised, no conversion tracking, one keyword board match, ill-defined objectives.

Paid LinkedIn (ABM) + events

  • ABM running on targeted accounts in the US, but not including all relevant titles.
  • ~40 events globally per year, spend over €200K (74% of total budget).
  • Event process not designed to maximise outcome; ROI not calculated or tracked.
  • One e-book campaign per quarter; campaigns not always linked to sales priorities or ABM.
Continue
  • Run relevant events in key sales areas.
  • Maintain SEO positions on branded keywords.
Stop / Start
  • Double down on SEO non-brand.
  • Invest wisely in SEA.
  • Treat events as campaigns, not isolated activities.
  • Improve and enhance ABM programs.
  • Add lead scoring with two flows: low-qualified (watch a demo) and high-qualified (book a meeting).
  • Enrich Salesforce data with web data using third-party tools.

DProduct marketing
Strong feature-benefit logic, but packaging, pricing, and buyer-persona depth are not yet there. The solution reads "robust but overwhelming" to SMB segments.

General + positioning

  • Thynk is lacking product marketing development.
  • Packaging, pricing, and messaging need to be redesigned to be easier to comprehend and sell, and to create upsell opportunities.
  • Pricing structure is not straightforward, internally or externally.
  • Current packaging can be limiting for sales (Groups & Events for example).
  • Individuals own some aspects of product marketing, but there is a lack of strategy. Competitive analysis not done on a regular basis.
  • Product knowledge missing in both Marketing and Sales teams.
  • Solutions based on 4 use cases (Lobby, Corporate & FIT, Groups & Events, Guest); good feature-benefit link established.
  • Solution perceived as robust but possibly overwhelming, especially for SMBs who may not be ready for it.

Buyer persona + reviews + advocacy

  • Missing detailed buyer persona research and documentation.
  • Good ranking on Hotel Tech Report (awards finalist in 2 categories); not ranked on G2, Gartner, TrustRadius.
  • Numerous relevant quotes in the pitch and on the website; missing video case studies.
  • Need to ensure client testimonies are fully leveraged across all MKT-Sales activities.
Continue
  • Maintain ranking on Hotel Tech Report.
  • Highlight benefits mapped to pain points.
  • Build a customer stories database.
Stop / Start
  • Reconsider packaging and pricing.
  • Revise product messaging.
  • Research the buyer persona; create a comprehensive doc shared with marketing and sales.
  • Generate reviews for G2 and other platforms.
  • Develop a more efficient process for client advocacy to maximise impact.
  • Video case studies.

FTeam, process & data
The headline finding. There is lack of trust, process, and data — and this gap, more than any tactical fix, is what holds growth back.

Marketing team & budget

  • Severely understaffed: only 3% of the workforce (6%+ at competitors). Smallest growth projection to 2025.
  • Budget limited, includes full-time staff. Limited third-party support.
  • Marketing strategy is "light," not data-driven, not quantitative. Unclear how success or ROI are measured.
  • Lack of trust between the marketing team and its stakeholders.
  • Team lacks some areas of expertise — mentoring and training will help.

Processes + data

  • Need clear, effective processes between Sales ⇔ Marketing ⇔ Product: LG, content validation, events, product roadmap.
  • US form sends an email (performance not guaranteed); EU form books a meeting directly (scalability not guaranteed).
  • Inbound leads not scored or segmented. Event leads followed up manually.
  • Missing documentation that is critical as the team scales.
  • Tech stack siloed: tools not integrated; HubSpot used mostly as a CMS, not fully leveraged by LG.
  • Salesforce → HubSpot one-way only (not vice versa). Contacts not enriched with behavioural data. Campaign field in Salesforce unused.
Continue
  • The pieces that work (LinkedIn content cadence, HubSpot baseline) — fix the integration, don't rip out the tool.
Stop / Start
  • Establish clear and effective processes for event, LG, validation, sales requests.
  • Solve the data problem by utilising HubSpot, or replace it as a marketing automation tool (not as a CMS).
  • Add data enrichment and lead scoring.
  • Organise the nomenclature used by teams.
  • Enhance collaboration between teams to restore trust.

08 — Priority list

Here is what I recommend focusing on. This list is by order of priority.

Five priority blocks. First two should be in motion within 30 days; brand and product marketing in parallel over 60-90 days; search as the compounding long-term engine.

1

Lead Gen

Rebuild the process for LG to get more quality leads and scale operations across geos.
  • Add a lead enrichment tool.
  • Look into a tool with better integration with Salesforce.
  • Introduce lead scoring.
  • Align on lead definitions.
  • Build nurture programs for non-MQLs.
2

Team & Process

Strengthen the alliance and collaboration between Sales ⇔ MKT ⇔ Product.
  • Redesign the event process.
  • Drive better lead generation and team synergy.
  • Regain trust.
3

Brand

Make the brand platform simpler, comprehensible, consistent, and scalable by redoing both copy and design.
  • Ensure that an external copywriter polishes the key components.
  • Redesign the brand to enhance it and elevate it to a world-class ENT brand.
  • Rebuild the messaging framework, aligning CEO, Marketing, Sales, and Product.
4

Product Marketing

Start doing product marketing from the ground up.
  • Conduct market research to align buyer and user personas, pain points, and competitive analysis.
  • Recreate the Product / Solutions / Feature matrix.
  • Reevaluate pricing and packaging.
5

Search

Increase consideration by securing visibility on non-branded keywords to expose the brand to new people.
  • Double down on SEO, starting with specific terms, moving into broader terms.
  • Align content production to SEO.
  • Invest in paid to complement SEO efforts.
