01 · Context and environment

Where Swan sits, from the outside

This is the framing slide, and it does most of the work. With the intake empty, I built it from public reading. Read the center column as a hypothesis to confirm, not as a fact you supplied.

Market
  • Embedded finance in Europe is moving from "fintech feature" to default expectation: software platforms want to issue accounts, cards, and payments inside their own product.
  • Regulation is the tailwind and the moat. PSD2 opened it, PSD3 / PSR are coming, and DORA (operational resilience rules) applies from January 2025. Holding an EMI license is hard, which is the point.
  • The buyer is rarely one person. A product lead wants speed, a finance lead wants unit economics, a compliance lead wants to not go to jail.
  • The category word is still unsettled: "embedded finance," "BaaS," "embedded banking," "white-label banking" all describe overlapping things to different buyers.
Swan (to confirm)
  • API-first embedded banking: accounts, IBANs, Mastercard issuing, and payment flows that other companies build into their own products.
  • Regulated as an EMI, operating across several European markets. The license is the differentiator competitors without one can't copy quickly.
  • Developer-led product surface: docs, sandbox, and integration speed are the first thing a prospect touches, before sales.
  • Institutional backing and named platform customers exist publicly. The logos are likely the strongest unused asset, same pattern seen across B2B SaaS.
Competitors
  • Stripe (Issuing + Treasury): the category gorilla, owns developer mindshare and distribution globally.
  • Mangopay: marketplace and platform payments, French-rooted, Advent-owned, a close direct comparable.
  • Treezor (Société Générale) and Solaris (Germany): European BaaS with banking-group backing.
  • Adyen and Marqeta sit adjacent on enterprise issuing and processing.

Sources: public web reading, May 2026. EMI / DORA / PSD timelines per published EU and ACPR guidance. Center column is a working hypothesis pending kickoff confirmation. Competitor scores in section 07 are flagged for verification.

02 · Business and marketing goals

The goals an audit needs, and what's missing

An audit anchors every recommendation to a stated business goal: the 12-month OKR, the revenue target, the markets that matter most. None of that was supplied. So the most useful thing I can do in this section is name precisely what's unavailable, because each gap is a decision you're currently making without data.

Decision data unavailable: business goalsNo 12-month OKRs, no current ARR, no prior-year to this-year growth rate. Without a revenue target, no marketing budget can be sized and no channel can be judged "enough."
Decision data unavailable: revenue by marketFor a multi-market EMI, revenue per country is the single most load-bearing number. Without it, budget gets split across France, Germany, Spain, and the rest by intuition, not by where it pays back.
Decision data unavailable: unit economicsNo CAC (cost to acquire a customer) and no LTV (lifetime value). The board will ask for payback math. Today's data can't produce it.
Decision data unavailable: marketing KPIs and budgetNo KPIs tracked, no monthly or annual budget, no agency or contractor spend, no MQL volume by source, no tech-stack ownership. A marketing function nobody can see is a function nobody can defend at the next budget review.
Decision data unavailable: pipeline and product usageNo funnel volumes, no stage-to-stage conversion, no sales-cycle length, no inbound / outbound ratio, no average deal value, no DAU / MAU. For a developer-led product, usage data is the growth signal, and it's dark.

This is not a scolding. It's the actual finding: Swan commissioned an audit, which means something feels off, and the first thing the audit finds is that the instrument panel is unlit. The good news is that this is the most fixable problem in the deck. It's section 06's first pillar and priority block one.

04 · Pillars, ideal world

What a marketing strategy for Swan should stand on

Five pillars. One sentence each. This is the ideal-world shape for an embedded finance company at Swan's stage, before tactics. The deep-dives in section 06 turn each into Continue / Stop-Start.

1. Measurement and process. The foundation every other pillar reports into: a defined funnel, KPIs the team and CEO see weekly, and clear ownership of the stack. Right now this is the hole everything else falls through.

2. Demand and pipeline. Repeatable lead generation across the markets that pay back, with cost per lead and source attribution good enough to defend spend by geography.

3. Product marketing and positioning. One category word, one buyer story per persona (product, finance, compliance), and a feature-to-benefit map that the website and sales both use.

4. Developer-led growth. The docs, sandbox, and integration path treated as a measured acquisition channel, not just engineering output. Activation is the metric.

5. Brand and trust. In embedded finance, trust is the product. Regulatory credibility, named-customer proof, and analyst presence are the assets, and they're currently underused.

10 · Priority list

Here is what I recommend focusing on, in order

The order is the deliverable. First block is highest impact, shortest time to results, and the precondition for everything below it.

1Measurement and process

Light the instrument panel. Until the funnel, the KPIs, and the per-market revenue exist, every other recommendation is a guess wearing a suit.

  • Define funnel stages and MQL / SQL definitions with sales, in writing.
  • Stand up one weekly KPI view the CEO reads.
  • Backfill CAC, LTV, and revenue per market.

Impact: high · Time: quick · Investment: low

2Demand and pipeline by market

Once you can see source and geography, stop spreading budget evenly and start funding what pays back.

  • Tag every lead by source and country.
  • Get CPL by market visible within the quarter.
  • Pick one inbound path and measure its form conversion rate.

Impact: high · Time: medium · Investment: medium

3Developer-led growth, measured

Your strongest channel is already running. It just isn't counted.

  • Instrument sign-up to first-API-call to activation.
  • Tie docs analytics to activation and drop-off.
  • Report activation as a marketing KPI.

Impact: high · Time: medium · Investment: medium

4Positioning: one category word

Three category words across your own surfaces split the buyer. Pick one and commit.

  • Choose the category word and apply it everywhere.
  • Write the feature-to-benefit map and a story per persona.
  • Move named-customer proof ahead of feature lists.

Impact: medium · Time: medium · Investment: low

5Brand and trust

Trust is the product in this category, but you can't manage what you can't measure. This comes after the panel is lit, not before.

  • Lead with the EMI license and the logos now, at zero extra cost.
  • Plan analyst presence once positioning is locked.
  • Revisit flexing brand to 20% of budget only after attribution exists.

Impact: medium · Time: slow · Investment: medium

The data isn't missing because it's hard. It's missing because no one built the place to put it. Build that first. Then this read becomes an audit.
