02 · The frame

This is a go-to-market audit, not a marketing audit.

Marketing cannot fix this alone, and it did not cause it alone. What you are looking at sits across three functions that have to move together. Where they pull apart, the business itself is at risk.

Marketing

The story & the engine

Positioning, brand, demand generation, product marketing, sales enablement.

Product

The product and roadmap

Architecture, roadmap, the merged platform, and building what the market actually wants and will pay for.

Sales

New logo + growth

New business, spotting the growth opportunities, and cross-selling and up-selling the base you already have.

None of the three works in a vacuum. Most of what follows is a failure of the alliance between them, not of any one of them.

06 · What your customers say

Your customers are already sold. The market never hears why.

From 27 recorded sales and customer-success calls. Three things came through again and again.

The product works

  • Strong, stable results in their own words: 57% email open, +58% conversion on personalised journeys, double-digit sales uplift
  • Satisfaction stays high even among clients freezing budget
  • The proof is real. It lives in 1:1 calls and is never packaged

They describe the moat, you don't

  • Customers explain the value themselves: "identify the hesitant visitor, then personalise"
  • That is the identity + personalisation edge, in their own words
  • No one names it as the reason to choose SaleCycle. It is in their mouths, not on the site

They leave for in-house, not rivals

  • The clearest churn: an 11-year client taking it in-house to cut cost, "not going to a different provider"
  • Adobe never came up. The real battlegrounds are in-house CRM and faster point tools
  • A value-communication gap marketing can close, not a product hole
Churn · enterprise services
It's purely in-housing the process instead of keeping it with you. The business saw a way to save costs. We're not going to a different provider.
QBR · e-commerce
What worries me is being stuck on the tool. Competitors deploy custom scenarios in minutes, no extra cost. With you it's bespoke, needs the tech team, and the delays are long.

The customers are not the problem, and neither is the product. The story, the speed, and how the value is packaged are. All three are fixable.

07 · The finding

One brand, one story, one platform: none of it exists yet.

The merger happened on paper, not in practice. The opportunity is to leverage the acquisition into one stronger market position. That is where 1 + 1 = 3. Today it is still 1 and 1.

One brand

Two sites still live, BEYABLE.com and SaleCycle.com. They split the traffic and stretch a small team to maintain both. No single brand the market can hold.

One story

No marketing architecture. Platform, product, feature, solution: undefined. The team is decorating a house it hasn't built.

One platform

Two separate systems, two logins, two different looks, no synchronisation between them. Still separated cogs, not one machine.

Everything this needs already exists inside the company. It just hasn't been put together yet.

08 · Three key takeaways

If you remember three things, remember these.

1 · Story

There is no single story yet. SaleCycle has one, BEYABLE has one, the market holds neither. No agreed answer to who the company is or what it sells.

2 · Visibility

Even a strong story is invisible today. Traffic has collapsed, search is near-zero, social and LinkedIn presence is thin. The story is not reaching the market.

3 · Lead generation

The funnel barely converts. Paid spend brings traffic that does not become leads, and the handover to sales is broken. The engine is not generating.

All three are fixable, in this order: get the story right, make it visible, then turn that visibility into leads.

09 · The way forward

Do it in order. Strategy before channels.

There is real motivation and real effort in this team. It isn't compounding because the work is happening out of order. Keep selling what is true and defensible: heritage, international reach, trust, and GDPR-native identity resolution.

Now · weeks

Foundation

  • Lock positioning and the marketing architecture (one brand, clear nomenclature)
  • Rebuild the website on the current brand: modern, clear, optimized journey
  • C-suite alignment first, then cascade internally
Next · this quarter

Relight & arm

  • Demand engine: SEO/GEO, funnel, tracking, MQL scoring, nurture
  • Sales fundamentals on a few winning products: battle cards, decks, the basics done well
  • AI operating layer so a small team can deliver more without growing
Later · the board's call

Decide & polish

  • Product integration roadmap (the load-bearing dependency)
  • Rebrand / rename decision, with a recommendation
  • Visual identity refresh, last, not first

Good motivation in the wrong order doesn't compound. In the right order, it does.

Deep dive B

Market & competitive review

The 2026 CRO and personalization landscape, where SaleCycle + BEYABLE sit against the field, and the opening no one else is standing on. This is the full review behind the highlight on slide 03.

SaleCycle, with BEYABLE, owns a sentence no one else in this field leads with: identify the anonymous visitor, personalise in real time, and stay GDPR-compliant without third-party cookies. As the cookie disappears, that is the difference.

The market behind it is real. Gartner sizes the personalisation-engine market at $1.2 billion in 2024, rising to a projected $31.6 billion by 2030 (a 20.9% CAGR), and McKinsey finds the companies that get personalisation right earn 40% more revenue from it, while only 15% of CMOs believe they are on the right track. The 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant names five Leaders: Adobe, SAP Emarsys, Insider One, CleverTap and Optimizely. They compete on suite breadth and cross-channel orchestration. None of them lead with identity, by which we mean identifying the anonymous visitor before personalising to them.

The proof is already there, on two fronts. The numbers: a reported 70% of anonymous traffic identified, and at Breitling (a SaleCycle client) 45% of previously anonymous traffic resolved, alongside a reported 30% uplift in conversions and 8% in average basket value. And the logos: SaleCycle carries a roster of global retail and luxury brands most of this field cannot, which is its own form of proof.

The gap is not the product. It is that the merger is real on paper but not yet one brand, one story, one platform a buyer can see, and the AI visibility starting to build is converting nothing yet.
