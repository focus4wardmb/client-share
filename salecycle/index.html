02 · The frame

This is a go-to-market audit, not a marketing audit.

Marketing cannot fix this alone, and it did not cause it alone. What you are looking at sits across three functions that have to move together. Where they pull apart, the business itself is at risk.

Marketing

The story & the engine

Positioning, brand, demand generation, product marketing, sales enablement.

Product

The product and roadmap

Roadmap, the merged platform, and building what the market actually wants and will pay for.

Sales

New logo + growth

New business, spotting the growth opportunities, and cross-selling and up-selling the base you already have.

None of the three works in a vacuum. Most of what follows is a failure of the alliance between them, not of any one of them.

07 · The finding

One brand, one story, one platform: none of it exists yet.

The merger happened on paper, not in practice. The opportunity is to leverage the acquisition into one stronger market position. That is where 1 + 1 = 3. Today it is still 1 and 1.

One brand

Two sites still live, BEYABLE.com and SaleCycle.com. They split the traffic and stretch a small team to maintain both. No single brand the market can hold.

One story

No marketing architecture. Platform, product, feature, solution: undefined. The team is decorating a house it hasn't built.

One platform

Two separate systems, two logins, two different looks, no synchronisation between them. Still separated cogs, not one machine.

Everything this needs already exists inside the company. It just hasn't been put together yet.

08 · The way forward

Do it in order. Strategy before channels.

There is real motivation and real effort in this team. It isn't compounding because the work is happening out of order. Keep selling what is true and defensible: heritage, international reach, trust, and GDPR-native identity resolution.

Now · weeks

Foundation

  • Lock positioning and the marketing architecture (one brand, clear nomenclature)
  • Rebuild the website on the current brand: modern, clear, optimized journey
  • C-suite alignment first, then cascade internally
Next · this quarter

Relight & arm

  • Demand engine: SEO/GEO, funnel, tracking, MQL scoring, nurture
  • Sales fundamentals on a few winning products: battle cards, decks, the basics done well
  • AI operating layer so a small team can deliver more without growing
Later · the board's call

Decide & polish

  • Product integration roadmap (the load-bearing dependency)
  • Rebrand / rename decision, with a recommendation
  • Visual identity refresh, last, not first

Good motivation in the wrong order doesn't compound. In the right order, it does.

09 · The rename question

Refresh the brand, or start fresh? I'd start fresh.

The merger left two confusing brands and a market that still sees the old SaleCycle. The board's real decision: refresh the name, or rename into one new company.

Keep & refresh "SaleCycle"

  • 16 years of heritage and blue-chip trust, kept
  • Operationally simpler, a refresh, not a rename project
  • But it still says who we were, and you keep
    re-educating: "we're not who you think"

Rename, a clean slate

  • One new company, erasing the two confusing brands in one move
  • Not just a new name, a new category we can claim
  • SEO is so low today that a rename loses almost no equity

The merger instability is itself the argument: a rename makes this one company, not two. Done right it is a category play, build the product, rebrand in parallel, and launch claiming new ground. And turning the page costs less than years of re-educating the market on "we're not who you think" (the Sendinblue to Brevo lesson). My lean is to rename. Your call.

10 · Three key takeaways

If you remember three things, remember these.

1 · Story

There is no single story yet. SaleCycle has one, BEYABLE has one, the market holds neither. No agreed answer to who the company is or what it sells.

2 · Visibility

Even a strong story is invisible today. Traffic has collapsed, search is near-zero, social and LinkedIn presence is thin. The story is not reaching the market.

3 · Lead generation

The funnel barely converts. Paid spend brings traffic that does not become leads, and the handover to sales is broken. The engine is not generating.

All three are fixable, in this order: get the story right, make it visible, then turn that visibility into leads.

Competitive review

The 2026 CRO and personalization landscape, where SaleCycle + BEYABLE sit against the field, and the opening no one else is standing on. This is the full review behind the highlight on slide 03.

The market is real and moving fast. The 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant names five Leaders, Adobe, SAP Emarsys, Insider One, CleverTap and Optimizely, and the frontier they are all racing toward is agentic orchestration: AI that builds journeys, content and experiments from a prompt. Insider, the one Leader SaleCycle is measured against, now leads with "the leading agentic customer engagement platform."

SaleCycle, with BEYABLE, owns a sentence no one else in this field leads with: identify the anonymous visitor, personalise in real time, and stay GDPR-compliant without third-party cookies. As the cookie disappears, that is the difference.

The remaining gap is not the product. It is that the merger is real on paper but not yet one brand, one story, one platform a buyer can see, and the AI visibility starting to build is converting nothing yet.

The honest read

Be honest about the gap that opens. On the generative and agentic frontier, SaleCycle is perceived a step behind, and that is worth taking seriously. The product work is real and worth doing. But this category is not won on agentic feature parity, those capabilities are converging and everyone reaches broadly the same place. It is won on identity, control, proof and speed to value, and that is where SaleCycle already stands apart: a reported 70% of anonymous traffic identified, 45% resolved at Breitling alongside +30% conversion and +8% average basket value, and a roster of global retail and luxury logos most of this field cannot carry.

It also shows up in how the platform is run. Most of the field hands a client a login and a dashboard and leaves them to optimise alone. SaleCycle runs as a managed service, on hand to make it work, the direct answer to the steep-learning-curve and developer-dependency complaint that recurs across the bigger platforms' own G2 reviews.

Deep dive C

G2 & reviews

Third-party review platforms are turning into a buying gate and an AI-visibility channel at the same time. SaleCycle is under-built on both, in a category where the leaders are not.

G2 is becoming both more central and more strategic for two reasons, and a third has just raised the stakes.

1. Enterprise buyers compare before they ever talk to sales

Enterprise buyers do most of their shortlisting on the review platforms before a rep is ever contacted, and the G2 Grid is the reference they read: the buyer-side equivalent of the Gartner Magic Quadrant or the Forrester Wave. If you are not positioned on the Grid, you are not in the comparison.

2. Reviews now drive AI visibility

LLMs treat G2 as a trustworthy, neutral source because the reviews are verified. Strong positioning and review volume on G2 lift how often a tool gets surfaced and recommended inside AI answers. The review base is no longer just social proof, it is GEO fuel.

3. One owner now controls where buyers research software

G2 recently acquired its biggest competitors, Capterra, GetApp and Software Advice. Four platforms now sit under one owner, so a single company controls a large share of where software buyers go to research. Presence on G2 is no longer optional shelf space.

Where SaleCycle sits today: thin and ageing on G2 (44 reviews) against competitors carrying hundreds to over a thousand. The full ratings comparison is in the competitive review, section 5. The fix is a deliberate review-generation motion, not a one-off push.

The marketing-to-sales handover barely exists

The handover from marketing to sales is not really built, and the tooling makes it worse rather than better. The signal is there, the team knows which lead visited which page and Slack fires an alert, but there is so much of it that the signal drowns. What a salesperson needs is the right person at the right time, ready to talk. Instead they get a flood of unqualified, not-ready leads, and they start to lose confidence in marketing-sourced leads altogether. That is the dangerous trend.

The fix: put AI and a scoring algorithm behind the alerts, so sales is notified for what actually matters, not for every page view. This is the MQL/SQL handover the quick-win plan builds.

Deep dive G

SEO & GEO visibility

Two different stories. Search visibility on Google is close to zero and has been abandoned. Visibility inside AI answers is quietly in a good place and rising. The second is the one to protect.

SEO is close to non-existent

Most pages are not optimised for search, and SEO looks to have been abandoned at some point: an agency that was expensive and, from what the team heard, not delivering. The result, Google refers almost no traffic on the keywords that matter and could convert. What organic traffic exists comes from branded searches, plus a handful of articles tied to SaleCycle's historical territory, abandoned cart: behavioural segmentation, cart recovery, omnichannel marketing strategy. The site structure itself, keywords, metadata, URL structure, is not built for search.

GEO is the surprise upside

AI visibility is actually in a decent place. SaleCycle is already surfaced in some prompts and is pulling real referral traffic from AI engines, rare for a company this size, and it is rising. The caveat: between January and May, none of those AI-referred visits converted into a lead yet. So the trend is genuinely positive and worth nurturing, but it is visibility, not revenue, until the site can convert it.

As AI search keeps taking share from Google, this is the channel to monitor and feed, exactly the territory where the GEO work compounds.
