01 · What I heard

Positive, in your words

A suite, not a collection of tools. More than a dozen acquisitions, one platform, the ambition of a European alternative to HubSpot, Salesforce or Klaviyo. Positive User since May: User.com, Sarbacane, Mailingwork and 4Dem on one foundation. €70M of revenue, mostly recurring, over 400 people in France, Germany, Italy and Poland.

The brand still needs work. Merge under Positive User while keeping the high-value local domains, and the fear of redirects. positivegroup.com points to User, which points to user.com. The positioning and the value proposition are not fully rolled out, and the rollout plan isn't written. Hard, but worth it.

The 40,000 Sarbacane customers stay on account.sarbacane.com, no forced migration. They are the ones living the name change first.

The marketing organisation. A team spread across several European cities, a CMO leaving on 28 September, a merger reorganisation carried out with determination, whose second half, bringing the teams along, is still to do.

What you expect. Leadership, structure, a complete reorganisation. A first phase as a fractional CMO, and we talk again.

02 · What I see

What the site already says

The architecture was decided in February. The rollout wasn't. On 9 February 2026 Positive published a brand platform: a "Positive [Product]" prefix for the primary brands, three verticals (User, Signitic, Surfer), and acquired brands "operating as endorsed brands" inside them. Seven months later, five product sites give four different answers to "how much Positive is in the name": sarbacane.com redirects to user.com/fr with a "Sarbacane becomes Positive User" page; 4dem.it folds into user.com/it/4dem; rapidmail.de carries "Positive rapidmail" in its header; nocrm.io keeps "noCRM" alone with a Positive tagline in the footer; mailingwork.de is untouched, not one mention of Positive beyond the careers link. Iconosquare sits in the navigation and outside the three verticals. A German customer and an Italian customer don't see the same company.

The promise changes with the language. "Turning reach into relationship" in English. "Nous créons des relations durables" in French. "La tecnologia che dà valore a ogni relazione" in Italian. "Faites de chaque contact le début d'une histoire" on user.com/fr. Two customer counts too: 40,000 companies on positivegroup.com, 45,000 on user.com. Each line is fine. Four of them is a brand that hasn't chosen its sentence yet.

The most differentiating idea is on no homepage. The May release calls Positive User "a decisive step toward an agentic CRM", with Uma as always-on agents that need no prompts. The group site calls Uma "an AI-powered co-pilot" across four products. That is the story a HubSpot or Klaviyo buyer would stop for, and it lives in the press section.

The site under the brand. A crawl of 120 of the 174 pages on positivegroup.com: 114 without structured data, 16 pages sharing 8 titles, 6,527 of 7,495 images without alt text. Nothing dramatic, and it matters more than it did in February: AI search engines read structured data first, and Positive sells an AI-search product.

The question that settles half of it: is the February choice the right one, and is Positive ready to go all the way with it? A masterbrand means the domains follow the name, eventually. Endorsed brands mean the names stay and the link to Positive is carried visually and in the story. Both work. Half of each doesn't: it confuses customers, it confuses prospects, and it leaves the market without a clear read of what Positive is.

Why it is urgent now. The €106M refinancing closed on 7 September exists to buy more companies. Every acquisition from here asks the same question on day one. The decision is no longer a cleanup of the last four brands; it is the rule for the next ten.

The useful precedent. I've worked inside both models: the masterbrand rollout at Criteo, with its prefix rules and its staged path for acquired brands, and the Sendinblue to Brevo rename in 2023, with the same questions Positive has today: the domains, the legacy customers, a multi-country team. I ran that one from A to Z. I know where it breaks, and in what order you keep it from breaking.

03 · The audit

Two subjects, one decision

01
Brand architecture

Scope: the six brands, the domains, the 40,000 Sarbacane accounts, the five countries. Deliverable: the architecture decision among the four textbook options (masterbrand, sub-brands, endorsed brands, freestanding), the staged path for acquired brands (endorsed first, absorbed when, or never), the naming and prefix rules the next acquisition will follow, a rollout plan per brand and per domain (what gets redirected, what stays, in which order), and the one line every homepage carries.

02
Marketing organisation

Scope: the current team, its cities, its skills, what each country expects from the centre. Deliverable: the target model (a central function with local relays, the shape I worked in at Criteo, then Brevo), the roles and their remit, the sequence to get there, and the change management handled at the same time as the org chart.

03
The GTM brain, the deliverable you didn't ask for

Positive's own promise is AI that works without prompts. The marketing function should run the same way. So the audit's decisions don't ship as slides only: they ship as a Claude-ready GTM brain, one repository holding the architecture decision, the narrative per brand, the vocabulary in each language, the ICP, the competitive read and the voice rules, that every team and every country drafts from. It is how five countries stop producing five promises, how a correction from sales in Poland reaches the copy in Italy, and how the marketing function keeps running when people change. I build it myself, in the same place the decisions live.

What you have at the end, concretely. Not an analysis and a diagnosis you then have to turn into work. Three layers, in order: the read (what is true today, with evidence), the decisions (architecture, organisation, the rules for the next acquisition), and the action plan (rollout by brand and domain, org sequence, first ninety days). We discuss it, we validate it together with Paul, and phase 2 starts on a plan that already exists. I hit the ground running; nobody waits for a strategy phase.
