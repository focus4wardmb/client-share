SmokeTest Inc: audit read.

Phase 1 + Phase 2 synthesis · 2026-05-29 · confidential

Headline finding

Your unit economics are strong. But you're scaling a funnel you can't yet explain, into a market you haven't yet planned for.

Built on the intake questionnaire only. The CEO interview was not conducted, so the strategic depth here is constrained: the Yes / But tension below is drawn from the data you gave, not pressure-tested against John's own read of the business. Treat this as a working read, and book the 60-minute CEO call before the priority list gets resourced.

04 · Pillars of a strategy

The ideal-world shape

Before the fixes, the frame. Given your scope (positioning, GTM, brand, demand gen, the marketing-sales motion), a strategy that serves a +71% target plus a market entry rests on four pillars. The deep-dives in section 06 work each one.

Pillar 01

Positioning and ICP

One named buyer, one documented persona, one defensible wedge. Speed-to-value and Slack-native are the differentiators worth owning. Lead with them and with the logo wall, not with feature lists.

Pillar 02

Demand generation and channels

A channel mix you can see into, source by source, with the MQL decline diagnosed before another dollar is spent. Organic protected, paid held to account, the UK pipeline built deliberately.

Pillar 03

Sales-marketing motion

A funnel where the leaks are named and instrumented. End-to-end MQL-to-won is 3.3% today. The job is to find the weakest stage and fix the handoff, not to pour more leads into a leaking pipe.

Pillar 04

Data, measurement and stack

A data layer joining HubSpot, GA4 and Looker so the next time MQLs move, the answer isn't "unclear why." This pillar is the quiet root cause behind half the findings here.

05 · KPIs and budget split

What to measure, and where the money goes

The five KPIs you track today are reasonable, but every one is a blended top-line number. The fix is segmentation, not new metrics. Three columns below: Growth, Brand, Team and productivity.

Budget split: 80% growth / 18% brand / 2% team

My default for mature B2B SaaS is 85% growth / 14% brand / 1% team. I'm flexing it toward brand here for one reason: you're entering the UK, a market where you have almost no awareness, and your positioning has gaps that paid channels can't paper over. On your $540K annual budget ($45K/month), that is roughly $432K to growth, $97K to brand, $11K to team. The brand bump funds UK awareness and the positioning work in pillar 01. When the UK has traction, pull it back to 85/14/1.

Out of scope per the intake: paid media management, product roadmap, customer success ops. The numbers above sit inside the in-scope demand-gen and brand functions.

Pillar 01

Positioning and ICP

Observations

  • The differentiation is sharp and verifiable: 7-minute time-to-first-insight vs a 45-minute median, Slack-native real-time alerts, flat pricing under $10K ACV. This is a real wedge, rare in this category.

  • Segments: mid-market (50-500 emp) is 70% of revenue, SMB 20%, enterprise 10% and growing.

Stop / Start

  • Reconcile the ICP gap. Decide whether your best revenue comes from the stated 50-500 segment or from the larger product-led logos, then aim messaging at the winner.
  • Stop describing the product before the customer. Lead the site and the deck with the logo wall; that proof is stronger than any feature list.

Pillar 02

Demand generation and channels

Observations

  • MQLs fell from ~210/month in Q4 2025 to ~180 today, reason unknown. This is the single most urgent finding in the audit.
  • Source mix: organic ~40% (growing), paid search ~25% (flat despite higher spend), content downloads ~20%, events ~10%, referral ~5%.
  • Paid performance: Google Ads $18K/month, ~45 leads, ~$400 CPL. LinkedIn Ads $4K/month, ~12 leads, ~$330 CPL.

Continue

  • Stop scaling Google Ads on flat returns. At $400 CPL with flat share, the incremental dollar isn't working. Hold the budget and reallocate.
  • Fix the 0.9% newsletter signup or remove it. At that rate the footer form is noise, not a channel.
  • Build the SEO and SEA analysis that doesn't exist yet (see the gap note below).

Pillar 03

Sales-marketing motion

Observations

  • The funnel: 180 MQL → 58 SQL (32%) → 22 opportunities (38%) → 6 won (27% close). End-to-end MQL-to-won is ~3.3%, roughly 30 MQLs per deal.
  • SQL-to-opportunity at 38% is the weakest stage in the chain. That's where the biggest leak is.
  • Sales cycle: mid-market ~67 days, SMB ~30 days, CFO involvement adds 2-3 weeks. Blended ACV $36K ($48K mid-market, $18K SMB).
  • Gong is used by only ~60% of reps. Lead scoring is rule-based on ~15 criteria. The Salesforce migration is planned for H2 2026 but not started.

Continue

  • Keep the 24-hour SDR follow-up target and the rule-based scoring. A 32% MQL-to-SQL rate is respectable for B2B SaaS.

Stop / Start

  • Instrument the SQL-to-opportunity stage. At 38% it's the weakest link; find why qualified leads stall before becoming opportunities.
  • Get Gong to 100% of reps. 40% of your calls are invisible right now, and you can't coach what you can't hear.
  • Decide the Salesforce migration before H2, not during it. A CRM move mid-expansion is risk: either resource it properly now or postpone it past the UK launch.

  • Stop tracking LinkedIn by hand in Notion. Move it into the reporting stack or accept that it isn't measured.

10 · Priority list

Here is what I recommend focusing on

This list is by order of priority. The order is the deliverable. The first block has the highest impact, the shortest time to results, and the tightest fit with your +71% target.

1

Diagnose the MQL drop

Your top of funnel fell 14% and nobody knows why. Until that's answered, every other growth move is built on sand.

  • Pull GA4 and HubSpot source data, Q4 2025 vs Q1 2026, by channel, in week 1.
  • Isolate whether the drop is organic, paid, content or referral.
  • Name the cause in writing before any new spend is approved.
2

Build the data layer

The reason the drop is "unclear why" is that nothing joins your tools. This is the root cause under half the audit.

  • Connect HubSpot, GA4 and Looker so source moves are traceable.
  • Retire manual LinkedIn tracking in Notion.
  • Segment CAC and LTV by ICP and channel before the UK hire.
3

Lock positioning before the UK hire

Cheap to fix, high return. Positioning gates the UK bet and the VP Marketing search. A few weeks of work, expensive to leave undone.

  • Ship the VP RevOps and CMO persona docs.
  • Resolve the gap between your stated ICP and your actual top logos.
  • Lead the site and deck with the logo wall and the 7-minute claim.
4

Close the funnel leak

3.3% end-to-end means ~30 MQLs per deal. The SQL-to-opp stage at 38% is the weakest link.

  • Instrument the SQL-to-opportunity stage and find the stall.
  • Get Gong to 100% coverage so calls can be coached.
  • Settle the Salesforce migration before H2, not during it.
5

Reallocate paid spend

Google Ads spend rose while share stayed flat at $400 CPL. The incremental dollar isn't working.

  • Hold Google Ads budget; stop scaling on flat returns.
  • Move freed budget to channels with room to grow.
  • Fix or kill the 0.9% newsletter signup.
6

Build the UK demand plan

Highest payoff, longest horizon. Build the pipeline before the closer arrives, not after.

  • Map PulseMetric and NorthernQuant, your two UK-local rivals.
  • Stand up a UK-specific demand plan ahead of the AE start date.
  • Fund UK awareness from the flexed brand budget in section 05.

These are the moves, in this order. The +71% target doesn't need more spend yet. It needs to know why the number you already have is moving.
